## Rules for using these references

- Review **publicly accessible pages only**, and only during an approved work package that includes the review. Never log in, submit forms, or attempt to access member areas.
- **Do not copy** copyrighted prose, images, photographs, logos or other branding assets, code, or distinctive creative design.
- Use the sites for **analysis and inspiration**: describe ideas in our own words and build our own implementation.
- Never copy information about other troops' youth or members into this repository.
- All Troop 32 rules still apply to anything inspired by these sites, especially [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) and [CONTENT-GUIDE.md](CONTENT-GUIDE.md).

## What to look for

Areas where the references may offer useful ideas:

- design conventions familiar to nearby Scouting leadership;
- information architecture and navigation;
- recruiting content and calls to action;
- homepage organization;
- use of photography;
- presentation of Scouting values;
- information for prospective families.

## How to evaluate an idea

For each idea, ask two separate questions:

1. **Is it familiar?** Would Troop 32 leadership likely recognize and welcome it because neighboring troops use it?
2. **Is it good practice?** Is it actually a strong modern web-design or user-experience practice, for example clear for newcomers, mobile-friendly, accessible, and privacy-safe?

An idea can be familiar without being good practice, or good practice without being familiar. Record both answers, then decide:

| Decision | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Adopt** | Use the idea in our own implementation. |
| **Improve** | Use the idea, but make it better (for example, more accessible or more private). |
| **Combine** | Merge it with ideas from other sources. |
| **Reject** | Don't use it, and note why. |
